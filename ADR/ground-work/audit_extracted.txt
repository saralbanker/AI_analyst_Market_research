Quantitative Engineering Architecture Audit: Indian
Equities Framework

1. Executive Summary

Forensic analysis of the Research Corpus v1 reveals a deeply researched, multi-domain framework designed
for algorithmic swing trading in Indian equities. The corpus correctly identifies that systemic risk, data
corruption, and concept drift are the primary destroyers of quantitative capital. The architecture mandates a
strict delineation between probabilistic AI (used exclusively for semantic reasoning and sentiment analysis)
and deterministic Python execution (used for signal generation and trade routing).

However, critical gaps exist. The corpus assumes stable API access during tail-risk events, relies heavily on
third-party APIs (Upstox, Angel One) that have documented historical gaps, and proposes hybrid AI-assisted
signal weighting schemas without providing the mathematical loss functions required to prevent AI model
overfitting.

Final Assessment: The foundational architecture (DuckDB/SQLite, local-first, deterministic execution) is
Supported and highly robust. The integration of LLMs for sentiment analysis is Partially Supported,
carrying high integration risk.

3. Domain-by-Domain Analysis

Domain A: Storage & Infrastructure
Concepts: Local-first, hybrid DuckDB/SQLite, Litestream WAL replication.

Constraints: Zerodha Kite API rate limits (10 orders/sec, 3 historical requests/sec) enforce asynchronous data
daemon requirements.

  Verdict: Supported. The combination of embedded transactional (SQLite) and vectorized columnar (DuckDB/
  Parquet) storage directly mitigates the computational bottlenecks of row-based databases while maintaining
  ACID compliance.

Domain D: Execution & Risk Management
Concepts: Non-ergodicity, dynamic margin, concept drift, walk-forward testing.

Findings: Systematic positive feedback loops (e.g., automated volume-based selling) cause liquidity
evaporation. AI models that anchor to peak valuations during regime shifts cause catastrophic losses.

  Verdict: Supported. Hard kill-switches and probabilistic backtest overfitting (PBO) calculations are absolute
  requirements for live trading.

20. Final Research Verdict

The uploaded corpus presents a highly defensive, skeptical, and robust framework for quantitative trading. It
accurately identifies that most retail and institutional capital is destroyed not by poor signal generation, but
by hidden technical debt, unverified API data, and catastrophic operational feedback loops.

The architecture's mandate to isolate AI exclusively to the "Research/Cognitive" domain while locking
mathematical execution, data storage, and order routing in deterministic, embedded SQL/Python
environments is the strongest and most validated claim in the corpus. Attempting to build an "Auto-Coder" or
fully autonomous AI trading bot within the constraints of Indian Equities (SEBI/Zerodha) will definitively
trigger catastrophic failure.
